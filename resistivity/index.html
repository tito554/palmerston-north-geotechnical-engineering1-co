Palmerston North sits on a dynamic alluvial plain where the Manawatu River has deposited layers of gravel, silt, and soft clay over millennia. The water table here sits high—often just 1.5 to 3 metres below the surface in areas like Kelvin Grove and Milson—which makes understanding subsurface conductivity critical before breaking ground. Our electrical resistivity testing cuts through the guesswork. We run Vertical Electrical Sounding (VES) arrays that map stratigraphy without the need for aggressive drilling in the early phase. For builders dealing with variable river terrace deposits, this non-invasive method reveals where competent gravels transition into compressible silts, helping to target test pits only where they add real value rather than scattering them across the entire site.

Electrical resistivity in Palmerston North is not about finding water—it is about distinguishing between water-bearing gravels and genuinely compressible clays before the excavator arrives.

Technical details of the service in Palmerston North

A common mistake we see in the Manawatu is assuming that resistivity lows always mean saturated clay. Palmerston North has pockets of saline groundwater near the old Hokowhitu lagoon deposits that can mimic fine-grained layers on a raw pseudosection. Without proper inversion modeling and local calibration, you risk misinterpreting a wet gravel as a fat clay and over-designing your foundation. Our lab runs a Schlumberger array with expanding electrode spacing up to 200 metres, pushing current deep enough to image the contact between the Rangitikei Sandstone basement and the overlying Quaternary cover. Each sounding gets tied to a physical log from SPT drilling or machine-dug pits so we can constrain resistivity-to-lithology transforms with site-specific precision rather than generic textbook curves. The result is a ground model that actually reflects the braided river paleochannel architecture underneath your project.

Critical ground factors in Palmerston North

The contrast between the river-proximal Fitzherbert side and the older terrace surfaces in Takaro is stark. Fitzherbert profiles often show a thick gravel package with moderate resistivity—good bearing, low settlement risk, but tricky to differentiate from a boulder-rich silt if you only look at the raw apparent resistivity numbers. Takaro, on the other hand, hides discontinuous lenses of peat and organic silt within the first six metres that yield resistivity values below 15 ohm-m, a range that can easily be misread as a perched water table. Ignoring these subtle differences during a site investigation means the difference between a straightforward shallow footing design and an expensive deep-pile retrofit six months into construction. Running VES at multiple azimuths on the same pad helps us catch lateral anisotropy that a single borehole would miss entirely.

Our services

Our resistivity work in Palmerston North covers everything from preliminary desktop reviews to full inversion reporting. Each survey gets tailored to the specific geological question the project raises.

1D Vertical Electrical Sounding (VES)

Deep point-soundings using the Schlumberger configuration to map layer thickness and resistivity. Ideal for foundation depth assessment and groundwater profiling beneath proposed building pads.

2D Electrical Resistivity Tomography (ERT)

Multi-electrode profiles that image lateral changes in the subsurface. We use this along proposed pipeline routes and embankment alignments where river channel boundaries need to be traced.

Combined Geophysical & Geotechnical Packages

Resistivity lines paired with targeted SPT boreholes and laboratory testing. We constrain the geophysical inversion with physical soil data so the final ground model is defensible under NZS 3404 requirements.

FAQ

How much does a VES survey cost for a residential section in Palmerston North?

For a standard residential lot requiring three to five soundings with full inversion and a written report, budgets typically fall between NZ$950 and NZ$1,510. The spread depends on electrode access, line clearance requirements, and whether we need to mobilize additional calibration boreholes.

What depth can electrical resistivity reach in Manawatu soils?

With a maximum AB/2 spread of 200 metres, we routinely image down to 50 or 60 metres. The actual penetration depends on the near-surface resistivity; highly conductive clays near the surface attenuate signal faster than dry gravels do, so we adjust the array geometry on site to optimize depth resolution.

Does high rainfall affect resistivity readings in Palmerston North?

Surface moisture after rain can lower contact resistance at the electrodes, which actually improves data quality. However, the deeper current paths that control VES interpretations are largely unaffected by a few days of wet weather because the investigation volume extends well below the seasonal moisture-fluctuation zone.

Can you tell the difference between gravel and clay from resistivity alone?

Not purely from a single resistivity value—both saturated gravel and soft clay can read low. That is why we calibrate each sounding against at least one physical borehole or test pit log from the same site. Once we have that tie point, the resistivity profiles become a powerful tool for interpolating stratigraphy between boreholes.

How long does a typical resistivity survey take on site?

A single VES sounding takes roughly 30 to 45 minutes to lay out, acquire, and check data quality. For a site with five soundings, expect our crew to be on the ground for a full day including mobilization and demobilization. Larger 2D lines may require two days depending on total profile length.
